# Deductions and credits

The difference between a deduction and a credit, and the baseline rule for business
expenses. Specific amounts, phase-outs, and eligibility must be confirmed against the
current IRS source.

## Deduction vs. credit

- A **deduction** reduces taxable income; its value depends on the marginal rate.
- A **credit** reduces tax owed dollar-for-dollar. A **refundable** credit can pay out
  beyond tax owed; a **nonrefundable** one only reduces tax to zero.

## Ordinary and necessary (business expenses)

Under **IRC §162(a)**, a trade-or-business expense is deductible if it is **ordinary**
(common and accepted in the field) and **necessary** (helpful and appropriate). Key limits:

- **Capital expenditures** — assets with a useful life beyond the year — are generally
  **capitalized and recovered through depreciation** (see depreciation.md), not expensed
  all at once.
- Some categories are **partially limited** (e.g. meals) or **disallowed** (e.g. most
  entertainment; fines and penalties). Confirm the current treatment per category.
- Every expense must be **substantiated** (see recordkeeping-and-substantiation.md).

## Standard vs. itemized (individuals)

An individual claims the **greater of** the standard deduction or total itemized deductions
(Schedule A: state/local taxes within the cap, mortgage interest, charitable gifts, and
others). Standard-deduction amounts change yearly — verify the current figure.

## IRS source

Verify against **IRC §§162, 63**, **Pub 535 (business expenses)**, **Pub 17**, and the
Schedule A / Schedule C instructions. Amounts and category limits are year-specific.
