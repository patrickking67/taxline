# Recordkeeping and substantiation

A deduction is only as good as the records behind it. General standards below; verify the
specifics.

## The general rule

Taxpayers must **keep records** sufficient to substantiate the income, deductions, and
credits claimed, and produce them on examination. The burden of substantiation is generally
on the taxpayer.

## Heightened substantiation (§274)

Certain expenses carry **stricter** substantiation under **IRC §274(d)** — notably travel and
listed property. The record should capture **amount, time, place, and business purpose** (and
the business relationship, where relevant). Inadequate records can disallow an otherwise valid
expense.

## Retention

Keep records while they remain relevant to a return — generally until the **period of
limitations** for that return closes. Keep records that establish **property basis** longer,
until the property is disposed of and the gain/loss period closes. Confirm the applicable
period.

## IRS source

Verify against **IRC §6001** (records) and **§274(d)** (heightened substantiation), **Pub 583**
(starting a business / recordkeeping), and **Pub 463** (travel and related expenses).
